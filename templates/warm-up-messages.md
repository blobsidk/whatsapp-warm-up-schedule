# Warm-up message examples

Warm-up chats are normal two-way conversations with people who know the number: colleagues, friends, family, your own other numbers, or partners who agreed to warm up with you. They are not for strangers.

## How to use these

- Use them as ideas. Write in your own words where you can.
- Never send the same line to many people.
- Wait for a reply before you send the next message. A chat that only goes one way is not warm-up.
- Spread chats across the day. Use random gaps, not a fixed interval.
- These people know you, so warm-up messages do not need an opt-out line. Every first message to someone new does. See [first-contact-messages.md](first-contact-messages.md).

`{your_name}` is a placeholder. Replace it before you send.

## Starting a chat

- Morning! Are you around later today?
- Hi, it's {your_name}. This is my new number. Please save it.
- Quick one: is Thursday still good for you?
- Can you send me that file here? This is my new number.

## Keeping it going

- Ha, I thought the same.
- Sounds good. What time works for you?
- I'll check and come back to you this afternoon.
- Thanks for sending that. I'll read it tonight.

## Questions that invite a reply

- What did you think of the meeting?
- Any good lunch places near the office?
- Are you still using that app you told me about?
- How did the move go?

## Ending a chat

- Perfect, talk tomorrow.
- Great, see you then.
- Thanks, have a good evening.

## What not to do

- Do not paste a list of lines into many chats at once.
- Do not add people to groups without asking first.
- Do not forward chain messages.
- Do not use warm-up to send offers or links to people who did not ask for them.
